The Physical Layer

How computers turn bits into electrical, optical, and radio signals, and how those signals travel across a network medium.

FOUNDATIONCCNA 200-301 ALIGNMENTREAD · UNDERSTAND · TEST

  • Describe the purpose of OSI Layer 1 and what it does not do.
  • Explain how data is represented as signals and bits on a medium.
  • Compare copper, fiber-optic, and wireless transmission.
  • Recognize common Ethernet copper and fiber connector/transceiver families.
  • Identify physical-layer causes of connectivity problems.

1. What is the Physical Layer?

The Physical Layer (Layer 1) is the lowest layer of the OSI reference model. It defines how raw bits are represented and transmitted across a physical medium. It describes the electrical, optical, or radio characteristics that allow two directly connected interfaces to exchange a signal.

Think of Layer 1 as the road, not the delivery address or the package label. It provides the path and signalling method. It does not decide which device should receive an Ethernet frame, what IP address a packet targets, or which application should process data.

Bits

The logical 0s and 1s that higher layers ultimately need to move across the link.

Signals

Physical changes such as voltage, light pulses, or radio waves used to encode information.

Medium

The material or space through which the signal travels: copper, glass/plastic fiber, or air.

Interface

The transceiver and physical circuitry that generate, send, receive, and interpret signals.

Bits are not tiny objects travelling down a cable

A bit is an abstract binary value. A physical interface maps bit patterns to a defined signalling scheme. On copper Ethernet, the interface uses electrical signalling. Fiber uses light. Wi-Fi uses radio-frequency electromagnetic waves. The receiver detects the signal and recovers the transmitted bit stream using the standard's encoding and timing rules.

Physical-layer specifications

Layer 1 standards define details such as data rates, connector types, cable characteristics, maximum segment lengths, signalling, pin assignments, wavelengths, and optical power budgets. Two devices can have compatible Ethernet configurations and still fail to communicate if the physical interfaces, optics, or media are incompatible.

This simplified diagram shows the physical part of a wired link. The transmitting interface converts bits into a signal, the medium carries that signal, and the receiving interface recovers the bits.

Simplex, half-duplex, and full-duplex

ModeDirectionExample / note
SimplexOne direction onlyOne-way broadcast or sensor transmission.
Half-duplexBoth directions, but not simultaneouslyShared medium where devices take turns transmitting.
Full-duplexBoth directions simultaneouslyTypical modern switched Ethernet links.

Duplex mismatch can cause severe performance problems. A full-duplex interface paired with a half-duplex interface may experience collisions on the half-duplex side, late collisions, retransmissions, and poor throughput. Modern switched Ethernet links normally use full-duplex operation.

3. Transmission media

Twisted-pair copper

Twisted-pair Ethernet cable contains insulated copper conductors arranged in twisted pairs. Twisting helps reduce electromagnetic interference and crosstalk. Common Ethernet cabling uses RJ-45-style 8P8C modular connectors. Category ratings such as Cat 5e, Cat 6, and Cat 6A specify performance characteristics; the supported speed and distance depend on the Ethernet standard and installation quality.

  • UTP: Unshielded twisted pair; common in office Ethernet installations.
  • STP / shielded cabling: Uses shielding to reduce certain forms of interference, but requires correct installation and grounding.
  • Common limit: A standard copper Ethernet channel is commonly designed for up to 100 metres, including the permanent link and patch cords. The exact limit depends on the Ethernet PHY and cabling standard.

Fiber-optic cable

Fiber carries information as pulses of light through a glass or plastic core. It is immune to electromagnetic interference and supports high bandwidth over longer distances than typical copper Ethernet links. Fiber is commonly used between wiring closets, buildings, data centers, and high-speed network devices.

Multimode fiber (MMF)

Larger core; commonly used for shorter-reach links, often within buildings or data centers.

Single-mode fiber (SMF)

Smaller core; supports long-distance transmission and is common for campus and provider links.

Wireless

Wireless LANs use radio-frequency signals rather than a guided cable. Performance depends on frequency band, channel width, modulation, signal strength, interference, obstacles, and the capabilities of both the access point and client. A strong signal does not guarantee high throughput if the channel is congested or noisy.

Common optical transceivers

Small Form-factor Pluggable (SFP) and related modules convert electrical signals inside a device to optical or electrical signals appropriate for a link. Match the transceiver type, speed, wavelength, fiber type, connector, and supported reach at both ends. A physically fitting module is not automatically compatible.

Performance and physical faults

Bandwidth is the link's capacity or data rate, usually expressed in bits per second. Throughput is the achieved rate of successful data delivery. Latency is the time required for data to travel from source to destination. Jitter is variation in packet delay. Attenuation is signal loss as it travels through a medium.

Common Layer 1 problems include a disconnected or damaged cable, incorrect pinout, dirty fiber connector, unsupported transceiver, wrong fiber type, excessive cable length, poor termination, radio interference, and a disabled or failed interface. Start troubleshooting by checking link lights, interface state, cable seating, media compatibility, and interface counters.

How this appears on Cisco IOS XE

Physical-layer checks often begin with interface status and counters. The exact output varies by platform and interface type.

show interfaces gigabitEthernet 0/0/0
show ip interface brief
show controllers ethernet-controller

Look for an interface that is up/up, expected speed and duplex, increasing error counters, and signs of a physical link problem. The controller command is platform-dependent and may not be available on every IOS XE device.
