1. Core concepts

A wireless LAN connects stations to an access point, which bridges wireless traffic to the wired network. A WLAN is identified by an SSID. A BSS is associated with one AP radio; an ESS can provide a larger WLAN across multiple APs. Lightweight APs may be centrally managed by a WLC using CAPWAP.

2. How it works

2.4 GHz offers propagation but fewer non-overlapping channels and more interference. 5 GHz offers more channel capacity. 6 GHz is available to Wi-Fi 6E and later devices where permitted. Channel width trades peak throughput against spectrum reuse and interference. WPA2/WPA3 are modern security; enterprise networks commonly use 802.1X with RADIUS.

3. Worked example

A campus deploys multiple APs with the same SSID and security policy. APs connect to access switches and a WLC manages configuration. Clients authenticate, associate, obtain DHCP configuration, and roam based on client decisions and network support.

Common mistakes and troubleshooting

  • Check physical and interface state before changing higher-layer settings.
  • Verify both ends agree on addressing, VLAN, encapsulation, and protocol parameters.
  • Use operational show commands; configured text alone does not prove a feature is working.
  • Change one variable at a time and verify the result.
